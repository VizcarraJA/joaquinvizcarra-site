Ask a patient with Parkinson's disease to describe a picture. Record their speech. Run it through a classifier.

Can you tell whether they have mild cognitive impairment?

Aresta, Battista, Salvatore and colleagues tried exactly this in a 2025 npj Parkinson's Disease study. They enrolled 70 Italian-speaking participants — patients with PD with and without MCI, and healthy controls — had them complete a picture description task, and used NLP to extract 35 linguistic features from connected speech. A support vector machine then classified patients into diagnostic subgroups.

AUC of 85% for PD without cognitive impairment vs. healthy controls. 83% for PD with MCI vs. controls. 75% for PD without MCI vs. PD with MCI — the hardest comparison, distinguishing two patient groups from each other.

The finding I keep thinking about is action verbs.

Action verbs — words describing physical movement — are processed by frontostriatal networks. These are exactly the networks compromised by dopaminergic neurodegeneration in PD. In this study, action verb production was negatively correlated with MDS-UPDRS III score in the cognitively intact PD group. More motor severity, fewer action verbs. The same circuits driving the motor phenotype appear to be driving a measurable linguistic deficit.

That connection matters because it links a speech variable to a biological substrate, not just to a symptom.

The caveats are real. The sample is small (70 participants). The data are Italian-only, and lexico-semantic features do not necessarily behave the same across languages. Replication in larger, multilingual cohorts is needed before this moves anywhere near clinical practice.

But the direction is worth watching.

Cognitive phenotyping in PD today requires formal neuropsychological testing — time-intensive, specialist-dependent, unavailable in many clinical settings. A brief picture description task, analyzed automatically, could eventually screen for cognitive decline during a routine visit or a telehealth encounter, without adding meaningfully to visit length.

The gap between proof of concept and clinical tool is still wide. The mechanism, though, is more grounded than most.
